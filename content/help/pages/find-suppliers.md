Sourcing searches the web for a product and reads the supplier pages it finds. Type a product, choose **Wholesalers** or **Manufacturers**, and press **Search**. A search takes a minute or two and costs about 20 cents. You can leave the page while it runs, and earlier searches stay in the list.

## What each row shows

- **Supplier:** a link to the page it read.
- **Type:** manufacturer, wholesaler, distributor, marketplace, retailer or unknown, with the words on the page that show it.
- **Based in:** where the business is based, not where the goods are made, with the words on the page that show it.
- **Price:** the price as the page gives it, what it buys and the unit price. No price is ever estimated.
- **Minimum order** and **VAT**, when the page says.

## Check before you rely on it

**Check every price on the supplier's page.** In testing, about 85% of the prices it read were right. A price can belong to another size of the product, or to a different bulk quantity.

The type and country come from what the supplier says about itself, so a trader can call itself a factory. Most factories don't publish prices, so you'll usually need to ask them for a quote.

## What it leaves out

- Pages about a different product, and price-comparison sites, are removed.
- Retailers are hidden until you turn off **Hide retailers**.
- Marketplaces such as Alibaba, and classified-ad sites such as Gumtree, are listed but not opened, so they show only a link.
- Some sites refuse to be read. The line above the table says how many.

## Limits

A search stops at 50 cents or after about 4 minutes, and the line above the table says if it did. A new search won't start if it could take the day past $5, counting searches still running.
